# The State of Broward's Transportation System

![](https://res.cloudinary.com/mindmixerprod/image/upload/w_1400,c_limit,f_auto/20181227225006-5a740c85.jpg)

Broward County's transportation system is shaped by policy and infrastructure investments informed by extensive engagement with members of the public. Together, we have crafted a vision for our future that guides our policy decisions and infrastructure investments.

## Many Broward County residents don't have access to an automobile. Our future depends on mobility choices for them, and all of us.

As we work to provide more choices for people to move around our region, we help zero vehicle households, the elderly, people who are disabled, and a growing cohort of people who do not want to own a car. Allowing people to move throughout our region without a car helps provide access to economic opportunity, attract a talented work force, and preserve our environment and reduce the impacts of climate change.

## Jobs Access is Highest in Dense Urban Clusters.

Land use and economic development decisions are not the direct responsibility of the Broward MPO, but they do impact our transportation system. When it comes to providing everyone with frequent, convenient transit access to job opportunities, creating dense clusters of employment and population is critical. [Click here to learn more about BrowardNext](http://www.broward.org/BrowardNext/Pages/default.aspx), the comprehensive plan and land use regulation document for Broward County.

## People who don't own cars spend less on transportation. That savings is a boost to the economy.

Car ownership is a significant cost to families and individuals. In Broward County, those without cars tend to spend less of their income on transportation. As investments in multi-modal transportation help to create more choices that allow households to reduce the number of automobiles required, that cost savings is spent elsewhere in the local economy.

## Most commute trips are in a single occupancy vehicle. That makes it harder for all of us to breathe easily. 
Broward County leads Miami-Dade, Florida, and even the United States average in single-occupancy automobile trips. This is bad for our environment, traffic congestion, and wear and tear on our infrastructure. It also negatively impacts our air quality and respiratory health, which hurts our most vulnerable residents.
