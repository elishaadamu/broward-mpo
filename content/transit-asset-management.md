# Transit Asset Management

Transit agencies are responsible for maintaining vehicles (including for passengers and administrative/maintenance use), facilities, and fixed guideway (rail and busways). In order to keep operations running smoothly and passengers safe and comfortable, it is important to keep all of these assets in a state of good repair. The below summary provides a detailed view of the number and quality of various transit agency assets from Broward County Transit and Tri-Rail. 



## Rolling Stock
A transit agency's rolling stock are the vehicles it uses to carry passengers to their destinations. The useful life of a transit vehicle varies from vehicle type to vehicle type. It is important to replace vehicles as they approach or reach the end of their useful life to ensure safety, reliability, and passenger comfort. ## Equipment
A transit agency's support vehicles are used by agency staff to support revenue (or passenger) service. Trucks and other rubber-tire service vehicles have a useful life of 14 years, while all other automobiles have a useful life of 8 years.

## Facilities
Transit agencies are responsible for maintaining administrative buildings, maintenance facilities, stations, and park-and-ride areas. Facilities are rated on a scale of 1.0-5.0 according to FTA's Transit Economic Requirements Model (TERM). Agencies that serve the Broward County area have established a goal of having no more than 30% of their facilities being at or below 3.0, or an "adequate" rating.

## Infrastructure
When a segment of transit infrastructure falls into disrepair, that section of rail or busway will have certain [performance restrictions](https://www.transit.dot.gov/PerformanceManagement), meaning that the vehicle has to drive slower than originally designed. The only transitway operating in the Broward County is the Tri-Rail system, which has 80 linear miles of guideway system-wide.
